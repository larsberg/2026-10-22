# Curtain & Table · 2026-10-22

A wall-plane tessellation study, continued from `../2026-20-21`. The cylinder and Yoshimura projection are removed. A shared-vertex XY plane is centered at the origin, with its normal pointing toward +Z. The starting camera looks straight at it from `(0, 0, 14)`; the existing orbit, zoom, camera controls, studio floor, lighting, materials, audio, recording, editable info, and Save/Reload workflow remain.

```sh
npm install
npm run dev
```

Run the dev process in the visible VS Code terminal. The fixed dev URL is `http://127.0.0.1:1234/`. Press **H** for controls. `npm run check` runs silent Node tests and the production build.

The default plane is 8 units wide and 6 high, with 12 × 9 subdivisions: 130 shared vertices and 108 quads. Size and subdivisions are editable under **Mesh → Wall plane** and persist through Save/Reload. The floor and its collision plane stay aligned with the bottom edge.

Four operation layers run in this order:

1. **Triangulate** chooses one diagonal independently for each base quad using the saved random seed, producing 216 triangles. **New variation** changes the seed.
2. **Dialate** creates a diamond across each shared edge and a triangle at each open boundary edge. The default result has 303 diamonds and 42 border triangles.
3. **Hexelate** splits diamonds along their short diagonals, then forms the face-center dual. This creates 346 polygon cells, including 233 hexagons in the default setup. Five- and seven-sided cells accommodate the random source topology; boundary cells preserve the rectangular outline. The result is a polygon tessellation rather than an all-hexagon grid. Disabling **Split faces within Hexelate** takes the direct dual instead.

4. **Smooth mesh** blends each non-boundary vertex toward the average of its connected neighbors. Four simultaneous passes at 0.5 strength are enabled by default. Pass count and strength are adjustable. Boundary positions and polygon connectivity stay unchanged. This result becomes the soft body’s rest shape.

Disable Hexelate to inspect the diamonds; disable Dialate as well to inspect the random triangles. **Apply operation layers** reveals the base quad grid when off. The operation layers preserve the flat rest mesh and its rectangular boundary. The base quads choose random diagonals, but their shared positions still begin on a regular grid. Hexelate favors six-sided cells and the smoothing pass evens their shapes; these later operations make the final pattern look more regular. New variation changes the diagonal seed. Wireframe shows actual polygon edges.

Additional passes are retained in a collapsed section between Dialate and Hexelate: face-center dual, fixed-boundary averaging, planar inset, subdivision/smoothing, and another diamond dual. They are off by default. The final smoothing pass after Hexelate can also be disabled. The existing 50,000-face limit and border ancestry protections remain.

Manual Triangulate, Dialate, Hexelate, Smooth mesh, and Undo remain available and act on the whole mesh. Face picking, selection controls, selection overlays, and the picking proxy are removed; dragging anywhere on the canvas orbits the camera. Manual Hexelate is one undoable edit, including its internal face splitting. Reset, Resize, and Reload rebuild the configured wall, clear manual edits, and reset the simulation. `window.__meshSketch.mesh.setMesh({ vertices, faces })` still accepts supplied polygon topology; procedural regeneration restores the plane.

A round aged-walnut café table and two walnut ladder-back chairs sit in front of the wall, with two white glazed ceramic plates. The furniture uses local grain, roughness and bump textures, turned and curved woodwork, worn edge details and small aged-brass fittings. The plates have modeled recessed wells, rolled rims and foot rings resting on the tabletop. The setting follows floor height and the existing environment palette, intensity and rotation, and casts and receives scene shadows. The table, two chairs, and two plates are five independent Cannon rigid bodies. Plates are detached from the table transform and remain on it through contact and friction. Compound cylinders approximate the tabletop, pedestal and plates; oriented boxes approximate individual chair members and swept table feet, preserving chair openings. Furniture collides with the ground, other props and the soft body. A shared 1/120-second loop exchanges impulses with nearby membrane vertex and face-centre sphere proxies; those corrections return to the deformable mesh while keeping its perimeter pinned. This is an approximate compound/sampled collision surface. **Mesh → Café objects → Rigid-body gravity** defaults to 98 (10× the previous 9.8) and spans 0–980. It applies to all five props and persists through Save/Reload. The Soft body Gravity slider retains its separate membrane setting. Grab strength follows rigid-body gravity. **Rigid-body friction** beside it spans 0–2 and defaults to the existing 0.55; zero allows sliding and higher values add grip against the ground, other props and membrane. It updates contacts without resetting motion, persists through Save/Reload, and leaves soft-body ground friction independent. Pause freezes both solvers.

Drag a prop with touch, mouse or pen to attach a point constraint at the touched location; off-centre grabs can rotate it. Release retains its motion. While holding, slide a second finger vertically, or use a mouse wheel, to change depth and push a prop into the wall. Empty-space and surface drags retain camera orbit. Pointer cancellation, lost capture, page hiding and focus loss release the constraint safely. Touch grabs resume playback and own the gesture from its start. **Mesh → Café objects → Reset café setting** restores the five original poses and clears velocity; Reset sketch also resets them. Floor-height changes reposition the setting; topology edits rebuild the collision samples. Save/Reload retains scene tuning rather than transient rigid-body poses. The existing contact shadows track grounded furniture and hide when it is lifted.

The **Soft body** controls enable a surface-particle simulation with stretch, bending, and rest-position springs. Every open-boundary vertex is fixed exactly at its rest position. A smoothstep pin weight decreases with physical distance from the nearest boundary edge, independent of subdivision density. **Pin transition width** sets the width of this gradient; **Edge pin stiffness** controls the graded rest anchors. The interior remains free to deform. Invisible triangle constraints support the polygon cells without adding visible wireframe diagonals.

**Pull toward camera** accelerates vertices toward the current camera position, so orbiting changes the force direction. **Gravity** adds downward acceleration in world space, independently of the camera. The slider spans 0–30 and defaults to 0; changing it preserves the current motion. Its value persists through Save/Reload. The camera’s follow target stays attached to the resting frame. Softness, bending stiffness, rest-shape stiffness, and damping tune the response. The solver uses fixed 1/120-second steps and limits catchup after a long frame. Pause and background suspension stop simulation time. **Reset motion** restores the current rest shape and clears velocity without rebuilding the operation layers. Disabling the soft body also restores the rest shape. The surface normals, wireframe, and shadows follow the moving vertices.

The ground collision plane follows the tile-top height, with 0.01 units of clearance for free vertices. Contact is resolved after all springs and anchors on every solver iteration, preventing those constraints from pulling the mesh below the floor. Incoming normal velocity is removed on impact, and **Ground friction** controls sliding. Boundary pins remain exactly at their rest positions, including the bottom edge on the floor. Collision remains active when the ground is hidden with its visibility control.

**Noise force and pinning** generates a GPU texture from three octaves of smooth 3D value noise, sampled at each vertex’s rest position. **Noise force strength** applies `(2 * noise - 1) * strength` as acceleration along the vertex’s area-weighted rest normal. High values push toward the front of the wall (+Z initially), low values push toward the back, and middle gray is neutral. This force moves vertices even with camera pull and gravity at zero. Its strength spans 0–100 in world units per second squared; increasing it increases acceleration without clipping. The fixed-step solver integrates it with the other forces, stretch, bending, damping, pins, and ground collision. Mobility fades the force near the edges, and every boundary vertex stays exactly pinned.

**Pin variation** separately blends from uniform anchor multipliers to the noise texture over 0–1. The texture multiplies the complete point-anchor stiffness (both the rest-shape stiffness and the graded edge anchor); it does not change particle mass or the edge-distance gradient. White preserves the configured anchor strength; black releases it. Setting Pin variation to zero restores multipliers of one while the noise force keeps running. Setting force strength to zero disables only that force. Disabling the noise field clears the force and restores multipliers of one without resetting motion. Older saves with pin amounts above one are clamped to full pin variation when migrated.

The shader exposes `uniform vec3 offset` and `uniform vec3 scale`, sampling `restPosition * scale + offset` with an accumulated bass-hit offset added to Z through `uniform float bassOffset`. **Z offset per bass hit** defaults to 0.35 noise units per attack; zero holds the field and negative values move backward. Audible bass note onsets come from the audio scheduler, and a bass-only 25 ms RMS envelope detects gated reattacks within held notes. Hysteresis and an 85 ms refractory period reject oscillator fluctuations and duplicate attacks. There is no time drift: silence, held notes, pause and background suspension hold the current slice. The manual Z offset remains the base value; a readout shows accumulated bass offset and hit count. The Mesh controls provide all three components for each vector. Changing Z offset moves through the 3D volume even on a flat wall. Scale sets frequency independently along each axis; negative values mirror the field and zero holds an axis constant. **Show pin multiplier** displays the actual applied multipliers as an unlit grayscale surface with a white pinned perimeter.

**Harmony controls noise scale** automates all three axes together. **Minimum noise scale** defaults to 0.4 and **Maximum noise scale** to 2. The controller reads the rhythmic gate programmed into the currently sounding harmony notes; six-step gated passages rise to the maximum, while flowing passages, harmony mute, or a change to an ungated sound return to the minimum. It follows the synth’s actual gate settings rather than overall mix loudness, and stays high through the gate’s rests. Transitions ease over 0.2 seconds on the rise and 0.45 seconds on the return; pause holds the current scale. The live readout shows the applied XYZ values. Manual XYZ controls are retained and become available when automation is off. Save/Reload retains the toggle, limits, and manual axes independently of the transient live scale.

The runtime exposes live Three.js uniform objects:

```js
const { offset, scale } = window.__meshSketch.pinNoise.uniforms;
offset.value.set(x, y, z);
scale.value.set(scaleX, scaleY, scaleZ);
```

Uniform changes are detected on the next frame (or by calling `window.__meshSketch.render()` while paused). A packed texture stores one texel per vertex: RGB holds the noise multiplied by an optional force texture and alpha holds the original noise; pin multipliers are derived from that original noise. GPU generation and CPU readback happen when the field or topology changes, including each bass-triggered offset change. Both the simulation and preview use these same values. Disabling noise, or setting both force strength and pin variation to zero, also stops bass offset accumulation. Reset motion retains the current field, and topology changes regenerate it from the new rest positions and normals. Reset sketch clears the bass offset and hit count. Save/Reload retains force strength, pin variation, base offsets, and bass step; the accumulated offset is transient. Old time-speed settings migrate to the default bass step. `window.__meshSketch.mesh.softBody.setNormalForces(values)` accepts signed per-vertex accelerations along those rest normals; the noise controller supplies them from the texture.

The optional image multiplier remains available through `window.__meshSketch.pinNoise.setForceTexture(texture, influence = 1)`. Its RGB pixels multiply the noise at each rest vertex; their product brightness drives outward normal force. Call `.invalidateForceTexture()` after rendering updated pixels into a render target. Passing `null` restores the original signed noise force. Pin weights remain driven by the original noise. The caller owns and disposes the supplied texture. There is no spectrum renderer or spectrum UI.

Audio is **More / Less**, an endless generative study inspired by the supplied Aloboi recording. The Strudel score in `src/raw-score.js` follows Dbmaj7 → F7 → Gbmaj7 → Gbm at 120 BPM. Its thirds/fifths use simple frequency ratios, with quieter seventh colours. A 72-bar arrangement moves through open fifths, full harmony, pitch glides, a sub-octave section, a filtered bridge, and gates/fragments. Later passes vary voicings and details without a fade-out. The six-step gate (`x x x ~ x x`) runs continuously on sixteenths against the four-bar harmony; it does not restart on each chord.

Three new band-limited oscillator instruments provide rounded harmonic bass, saturated ratio chords, and a gliding overtone lead. Each part has its own shared filter, saturation, room and beat-synced delay chain. The low end stays centered; upper notes have restrained stereo placement. Sparse percussion adds a half-time pocket: a short sub kick, a brushed snare on beat three, and two or three soft offbeat hats per bar. The opening uses fewer hits, each eighth bar drops the drums, and the filtered bridge leaves them out. The Drums slider controls the group; each part retains its own level and effects controls. The Audio tab exposes all six instrument channels and effects, and audio starts off. `scripts/render-raw-audio.js` provides a silent OfflineAudioContext render of these same voices and effects for signal checks. This is a new composition informed by spectral analysis; no source audio is sampled, and its perceptual similarity has not been checked by direct listening.

**Lighting → Environment reflections → Build light planes with music** controls seven reflection planes independently. Chord and bass onsets flash the front and lower planes for 0.18 seconds, with a dim sustained bed between attacks. Harmony density and synth brightness build the side plane; lead entries lift the rim and back. The highlight strip follows the actual six-step gate envelope, including its silent rests, while percussion and gates feed the accent. The lower plane sits below and in front of the wall so its bass accents reflect onto the visible side. All cues come from the sounding score and instrument gains, including channel mutes. Minimum and peak brightness default to 0 and 2.2, allowing unused planes to go fully dark while preserving palette colors, environment intensity, and rotation. Fade in and fade out default to 0.08 and 0.12 seconds and are adjustable from 0.01 to 0.3 seconds. A bounded rate follows moving gate targets without repeatedly restarting a fade; stationary targets are reached within the selected duration. Manual palette changes also take 0.3 seconds. Pause freezes the levels; Reset starts at the minimum. Disable the build toggle for the original steady panel levels. The seven panel sources and studio background are convolved once into eight radiance maps; per-frame GPU tinting and addition animate the individual sources without regenerating the environment.

`public/setup.json` stores the studio, mesh, soft-body, pin-noise and audio settings; it does not store a transient simulated pose. `public/sketch.json` uses the title **Curtain & Table**, the `2026-10-22` identity and October 22, 2026 creation date. The standalone and gallery player are packaged for GitHub Pages.

Camera orbit, zoom and restored views keep the camera at least 0.65 scene units above the tile floor. The limit follows floor height, preserving the focal point and orbit distance wherever possible.


## Recording and publication

The recording workflow matches `../2026-20-21` and `../2026-10-16`. Enable **Audio → Enable audio**, play the sketch, then press **R** to start/stop or use **Info → Record video & audio**. Keep the shared tab visible. Download the finished clip from the recording badge. The canvas and final stereo mix (including effects, master volume and limiting) are captured together, excluding controls. MP4/H.264/AAC is preferred; WebM is the fallback. Choose 30 or 60 fps; actual frame rate follows rendering performance. The long side is capped at 1920 pixels. Hiding the tab stops recording; the 256 MB limit bounds memory. Recording files stay local. The prepared 30-second export is [`reports/curtain-and-table-720p-30s.mp4`](reports/curtain-and-table-720p-30s.mp4): 1280 × 720 H.264 video at 60 fps with stereo 48 kHz AAC audio.

**Info → Capture thumbnail** saves the current canvas to `public/thumbnail.jpg` and updates the manifest's image path and exact dimensions. The build uses that same image for the gallery and Open Graph/Twitter previews. Capture after choosing a view and musical moment, then rebuild.

`public/sketch.json` supplies the title, description, author, creation date, tags, technologies, audio gesture requirement, thumbnail, planned repository/standalone URL and `sketch-player:v1` embed permissions. The composition is original synthesis inspired by Aloboi's **Give Me More (Just Rawer)**; the source MP3 is not bundled. The saved scene preserves its attribution. Cannon ES supplies rigid-body physics, while the membrane uses the local soft-body solver.

Run `npm run check` to validate and build `dist/`. The standalone package includes `index.html`, `sketch.json`, `setup.json`, `thumbnail.jpg`, `catalogue.json`, `site-entry.json`, bundled assets and `.nojekyll`. No development API is needed in production. Production Save stores viewer settings on their own device. Local recording exports belong in ignored `reports/`, outside the deployed package.
